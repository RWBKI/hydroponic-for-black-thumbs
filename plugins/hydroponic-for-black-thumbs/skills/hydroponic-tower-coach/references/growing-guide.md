# Growing guide

Everything needed to start the tower and keep it going: doses in millilitres, mixing order, how to stop draining the reservoir every week, moving plants from soil to hydro, noise, and what to do when things go wrong.

The numbers below come from one real tower. They were calibrated on one tap water and one EC pen, so treat them as a worked example and measure your own.

## Reference setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Tower | 24 sites, 4 columns of 6 |
| Reservoir | 37 L |
| Location | Outdoors under a clear-roofed shelter, open on three sides |
| Light | Natural only |
| Tap water | pH 8.0, EC 0.30 mS/cm, hardness 125 ppm CaCO3, calcium 33 mg/L |
| Nutrients | Terra Aquatica TriPart: Micro (soft water version), Grow, Bloom |
| Medium | Clay pebbles |
| Crops | Lettuce, lamb's lettuce, spinach, chives, basil |

TriPart is the European three-part line formerly sold as GHE Flora Series. Other three-part nutrients follow the same logic, but their doses differ.

## One reservoir, several crops

Every plant has its own EC range. With a single reservoir you aim for the zone they share. Lettuce sets the ceiling because it burns first.

| Crop | EC (mS/cm) | pH |
|---|---|---|
| Lettuce | 0.8 to 1.2 | 5.5 to 6.0 |
| Basil | 1.0 to 1.6 | 5.5 to 6.5 |
| Chives | 1.0 to 1.6 | 6.0 to 6.5 |
| Lamb's lettuce, cress | 0.8 to 1.2 | 6.0 to 6.5 |
| Spinach, mizuna, rocket | 1.0 to 1.6 | 6.0 to 6.5 |

**Shared window: EC 1.0 to 1.2, pH 6.0.** Leaf crops have similar appetites, which makes this about the easiest reservoir to run. Fruiting crops such as strawberries were dropped because they need a different regime.

## Know your water first

Measure plain tap water before adding anything. That EC reading is your zero.

In the reference setup the water is soft (EC 0.30, little calcium). Consequences:

- No reverse osmosis needed. The "soft water" Micro is the right one because it supplies the calcium the water lacks.
- A tap pH of 8 looks alarming, but soft water has almost no bicarbonate to hold it there. The nutrients do much of the correction on their own.
- The price of soft water is that pH moves. It climbs during the week as plants consume nitrates. Check it every two days.

Measured on this water: **1 mL/L of TriPart mix adds about 0.5 EC**, twice the usual rule of thumb. The manufacturer's doses (1 / 1.5 / 0.5 mL/L) gave EC 1.8, too strong for lettuce, hence the reduced doses below.

For chlorine, let the water stand uncovered for 24 hours, or run the pump for an hour before adding nutrients.

## Doses

For a 37 L reservoir. Scale by volume.

### Recovery and low-light regime: EC 0.7 to 0.9

Use for the first 10 days after transplanting, and all winter.

| Bottle | Dose | For 37 L |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | 0.4 mL/L | 15 mL |
| Grow | 0.4 mL/L | 15 mL |
| Bloom | 0.4 mL/L | 15 mL |

Roots that just lost their soil cannot drink yet, and feeding them hard burns them. Move to cruise when you see a new leaf at the heart and white root tips. In winter, a plant that barely photosynthesises barely eats.

### Cruise, leafy growth: EC 1.0 to 1.2

The default for most of the year.

| Bottle | Dose | For 37 L |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | 0.6 mL/L | 22 mL |
| Grow | 0.9 mL/L | 33 mL |
| Bloom | 0.3 mL/L | 11 mL |

Grow dominates because leaves are built from nitrogen. Bloom stays at a low dose for potassium and phosphorus.

### Top-up dose: raises EC by about 0.2

| Bottle | Dose | For 37 L |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | 0.13 mL/L | 5 mL |
| Grow | 0.2 mL/L | 7.5 mL |
| Bloom | 0.07 mL/L | 2.5 mL |

Always measure 15 minutes after. Never guess.

## Mixing order

1. Fill with rested water, 18 to 22 °C.
2. Add Micro, stir 30 seconds. Always first.
3. Add Grow, stir.
4. Add Bloom, stir.
5. Wait 15 minutes, then measure and adjust pH to 6.0.
6. Run the pump 10 minutes before putting plants back.

Never mix two concentrates together in a syringe, cup or bottle. Micro and Bloom undiluted form calcium phosphate: a white insoluble deposit, lost nutrients and a clogged pump. Each bottle goes straight into the full volume, one after the other.

### Adjusting pH

Do not dose pH Down from a formula. Add 2 mL at a time (about 0.13 pH per mL in 37 L of this water), stir, wait 15 minutes, measure again. In unbuffered water, 2 mL too many drops you to pH 4.5 and you spend the evening climbing back.

## Stop draining every week

The weekly drain is a beginner's safety net. It exists because without instruments you cannot know what is in the tank. Two things make it unnecessary: a bigger volume and an EC meter.

**Bigger volume.** Everything that goes wrong is a drift rate (pH rising, EC concentrating, water warming), and each rate is divided by the volume. More water also means more ballast at the foot of the tower. Use a siphon hose to drain; do not carry the tank.

**The rhythm:**

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Every 2 to 3 days | Top up to the mark with plain water adjusted to pH 6. Never with nutrient solution: plants drink more water than they eat salts, so topping up with nutrients concentrates the tank. Then measure pH, correct, and measure EC. |
| EC below 0.9 | Plants ate faster than they drank. Add the top-up dose, measure again after 15 minutes. |
| EC above 1.4 | They drank faster than they ate. Plain water only, and cut doses by 20 % at the next fresh solution. |
| Every 15 to 21 days | Full drain. Sooner if the total of your top-ups exceeds the tank volume. |

**Why you cannot top up forever.** EC tells you how much, never what. Plants strip nitrogen and potassium quickly and barely touch calcium and sulphates. After three or four weeks the meter can read a perfect 1.1 while the tank is a calcium soup with no nitrogen, and excess calcium blocks uptake of the rest. This is nutrient lockout, and it looks exactly like a deficiency. The drain resets the proportions, not the quantity.

**What you drain is not waste.** It is a complete, dilute mineral fertiliser. Water the garden with it. Soil buffers the ionic imbalance that bothered bare roots.

### Without an EC meter: the one-third method

Every 7 days, remove one third of the volume and replace it with fresh solution dosed for that third. Full drain every three weeks. This already cuts waste by about three.

## Light, position and the shelter

A clear roof with open sides is neither open air nor a closed greenhouse, and for a tower it is the best of the three: air circulates (no heat build-up, fewer spider mites, less botrytis), and rain no longer dilutes the reservoir.

Three things to watch:

- **Clean the roof.** A dirty polycarbonate sheet easily loses 30 % of the light. Twice a year, ten minutes.
- **You are not frost-proof.** On a frost night, switch the pump off and let the column drain into the tank. Thin hoses freeze first.
- **Wind still gets in.** Ballast at the foot of the tower is not optional.

Useful light for plants is measured as daily light integral (DLI, mol/m²/day). For lettuce: 14 to 18 means fast, compact growth; 8 to 12 means slow but correct; under 5 it stretches and waits.

Example year for the reference site (mild oceanic climate, estimated, not measured):

| | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun | Jul | Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov | Dec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DLI estimate | 5 | 7 | 10 | 14 | 17 | 19 | 19 | 16 | 12 | 8 | 6 | 5 |
| Lettuce | H | H | P | P | P | P | P | P | P | P | H | H |
| Lamb's lettuce, cress | P | P | H | – | – | – | – | – | P | P | P | P |
| Chives | H | H | P | P | P | P | P | P | P | P | H | H |
| Basil | – | – | – | – | H | P | P | P | H | – | – | – |
| Spinach, mizuna | H | P | P | P | H | – | – | – | P | P | P | H |

P = producing, H = holding (alive, slow, little harvest), – = off season.

Two actionable consequences:

- **Plant early.** One week of September light is worth three weeks of November.
- **Lower EC when light drops.** From November, return to the recovery doses. Unconsumed salts accumulate otherwise. Drains can be spaced to 3 or 4 weeks.

Cool nights are not the enemy: lamb's lettuce, spinach and mizuna taste better after a few of them.

## 24 sites are not 24 equal plants

The top site sees the whole sky. The bottom one sees a sliver and sits in the shadow of everything above.

| Zone | Light | What goes there | Per plant, full season |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top, 6 sites | 100 % | Head lettuce, and basil while it lasts | 180 to 220 g |
| Upper middle, 6 | about 70 % | Cut-and-come-again lettuce | 120 to 160 g |
| Lower middle, 6 | about 45 % | Lamb's lettuce, spinach, mizuna, rocket | 60 to 110 g |
| Bottom, 6 | about 30 % | Chives, parsley, chervil | Low volume, continuous |

Estimated yield over a full year: 15 to 20 kg of leaves, give or take 30 %. More if you harvest leaf by leaf.

**Stagger your plantings.** Never plant all 24 sites on the same day: 24 lettuces planted together are 24 lettuces to eat in the same week. Plant 4 to 6 every two weeks. From week six you harvest 4 to 6 plants a week, indefinitely. A tower that is never full is a well-run tower.

Plug empty sites with foam, a cap or wide tape. Light on water grows algae.

## Noise

About 80 % of the splashing comes from one place, and it is not the medium.

1. **Water falling into the reservoir.** Extend the return hose to 3 or 4 cm below the surface. This removes most of the noise.
2. **Too much flow.** A tower never needs the pump's maximum. Add a small valve, or a T that returns part of the flow to the tank below the surface.
3. **The pump vibrating against the wall.** Sit it on foam and keep pump and hose off the sides of the tank.
4. **Dripping between levels.** A wick (cord, horticultural felt, coco strip) along the column makes water run instead of fall.

Replacing clay pebbles with coco is not recommended in a recirculating tower: coco holds too much water and too little air (root rot), sheds fibres into the pump, and exchanges ions with the solution. Keep pebbles as the medium, fix noise in the plumbing, and use coco only as plugs at the collar.

## From soil to clay pebbles

This is the step where plants are lost.

1. Rinse the pebbles until the water runs clear, then soak them overnight in pH 5.5 water. New pebbles are alkaline.
2. Water the plants 1 to 2 hours before.
3. Work in the evening or in shade.
4. Dip the root ball in lukewarm water (20 to 22 °C), wait two minutes, then swirl gently. Never pull on the plant.
5. Change the water two or three times until no soil is left. Any soil left in the system ends in rot.
6. Cut off dead roots and damaged leaves with clean scissors.
7. Remove about a third of the foliage on larger plants.
8. Set the plant in the basket: roots hanging through into the flow, collar level with the pebbles. The collar must never be buried or soaked.
9. First five days: recovery solution, pump on continuously or 15 min on / 15 min off, filtered light, mist the leaves morning and evening.

Plants sulk for two or three days. That is normal. Do not strengthen the solution to help. Keep two or three plants in soil as spares.

Pebbles do not hold a bare-rooted seedling upright. A coco plug, rockwool cube or piece of sponge around the collar fixes that.

## Routine at a glance

| When | What | The detail that matters |
|---|---|---|
| Every 2 to 3 days | Level, pH, EC | Top up with plain water at pH 6, never with nutrients |
| When EC falls below 0.9 | Top-up dose | Measure again after 15 minutes |
| Every 15 to 21 days | Full drain | Rinse tank and strainer; old solution goes on the garden |
| After driving rain | Measure EC | Side rain can still dilute the tank |
| Always | Water temperature | 18 to 22 °C. Above 24 °C dissolved oxygen collapses and pythium moves in |
| Always | Keep light off the roots | Opaque, closed reservoir |
| Monthly | Calibrate the pH meter | pH 4.0 and 7.0 buffers. An uncalibrated meter is worse than none |
| From November | Lower the doses | Recovery regime, drains every 3 to 4 weeks |
| Twice a year | Clean the roof | Up to 30 % more light |
| Frost forecast | Pump off | Let the column drain |

## Crop notes

**Lettuce.** Easiest and fastest: three to four weeks after recovery. Harvest outer leaves and leave the heart. Bolts and turns bitter above 24 °C. It is the most salt-sensitive crop, so it is your early warning for the whole tank.

**Basil.** Likes it warmer and hates cold: leaves blacken below 12 °C. Pinch above a pair of leaves once it has three levels. Never let it flower. It needs the top of the tower.

**Chives.** The most tolerant once established and the most tedious to transplant, because the roots hold soil like a sponge. Divide the clump while rinsing. Cut at 5 cm, never flush. Goes at the bottom.

**Lamb's lettuce and garden cress.** They save the winter. Lamb's lettuce grows at 5 °C in light where lettuce stops. Cress is ready in three weeks and regrows after cutting.

**Spinach, mizuna, rocket.** Winter spinach grows at 4 °C and tolerates shade; mizuna is extremely productive per unit of light; rocket is fastest but slows most in December. All regrow from the heart.

## Troubleshooting

Where the symptom appears matters as much as the symptom.

| What you see | What it is | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Old bottom leaves yellowing evenly | Nitrogen shortage, spent solution | Drain and refill at cruise doses |
| Young top leaves yellow between green veins | Iron locked out, almost always pH too high | Measure pH. Above 6.5 iron is unavailable. Bring it back to 6.0 |
| Brown, dry, crispy leaf edges | Solution too strong | Drain and remake at 70 % of doses |
| Cloudy water, sewer smell, brown slimy roots | Pythium, root rot | Empty everything, clean tank and pump, get temperature under 22 °C, add oxygen, cut affected roots, look for leftover soil |
| Green film on pebbles or walls | Algae: light is reaching the water | Block the light |
| Small black flies | Fungus gnats: organic matter left in the system | Drain, clean, rinse roots better next time |
| pH climbing hard every day | Nitrate uptake, badly rinsed pebbles, or fresh tap water | A gentle rise is normal. Sudden and daily: suspect new pebbles, then freshly added tap water, whose bicarbonate takes 2 to 3 days to neutralise |
| White deposit at the bottom | Calcium phosphate precipitation | Two concentrates met undiluted. Check the mixing order |
| EC collapsing overnight | Rain got in | Top up nutrients back into the window; shield the exposed side |
| Growth slowing in autumn with good pH and EC | Light, not nutrition | Do not raise doses. Lower EC |
| Long soft stems, pale spaced leaves | Etiolation, not enough light | No fertiliser fixes this. Move the plant up or switch to shade-tolerant crops |
| Aphids, flea beetles | You are outdoors | Water jet or soft soap on the leaves only. Never a systemic insecticide: it ends up in the reservoir and on your plate |

## What you do not need

- Reverse osmosis water, if your tap water is already soft and low in EC.
- A fourth bottle (booster, root stimulant, enzyme, PK). A three-part nutrient is complete for salads and herbs.
- Coco as the main medium.

Consumables you will rebuy: pH calibration buffers (monthly), pH Down (a bottle lasts months), seeds.

## Sources and limits

Doses are derived from the manufacturer's TriPart application chart, adjusted downward for leafy greens and herbs. EC and pH ranges per crop are standard horticultural reference values. Light values are estimates from average sunshine and the geometry of one shelter, not measurements. Yields are orders of magnitude. Your EC meter has the last word, and your plants before it.
